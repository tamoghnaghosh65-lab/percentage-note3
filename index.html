Percentage: Basic Language

x% = x/100
  • x% of A = A × x/100
  • A is x% of B ⇒ A/B × 100 = x%
  • Percentage change = (Change ÷ Original) × 100
  • Increase: multiply by (100 + x)/100. Decrease: multiply by (100 − x)/100.
Example: 15% of 240 = 240 × 15/100 = 36.

Technique 1 Comparing two quantities

If A is x% of B, then B is what percentage of A?

B as % of A = (100/x) × 100 = 10000/x %
Example: A is 25% of B. B is what % of A?
A = 25% of B ⇒ B/A = 100/25 = 4 ⇒ B is 400% of A.

If A is x% of B, then x% of A is:

(x²/100)% of B
Example: A = 20% of B. 20% of A = 20% × 20% of B = 4% of B.

Technique 2 Increase / decrease in a number

When a number x is increased or decreased by y%, its new value is:

New value = x × (100 ± y)/100

Use + for increase and − for decrease.

Example: Income ₹8,000 increases by 20%.
New income = 8,000 × 120/100 = ₹9,600.
Example: Computer price ₹20,000 falls by 25%.
New price = 20,000 × 75/100 = ₹15,000.

Technique 3 Reverse comparison (more / less)

  • If X is a% more than Y, then Y is [a/(100+a)] × 100% less than X.
  • If X is a% less than Y, then Y is [a/(100−a)] × 100% more than X.
More → reverse less: a/(100+a) × 100
Less → reverse more: a/(100−a) × 100
Example: Ravi earns 20% more than Ram. Ram earns how much % less than Ravi?
Let Ram = 100; Ravi = 120. Difference/Ravi × 100 = 20/120 × 100 = 16⅔% less.
Example: Preeti's marks are 20% less than Vandana's. Vandana's marks are how much % more than Preeti's?
Vandana = 100, Preeti = 80. Difference/Preeti × 100 = 20/80 × 100 = 25% more.

Technique 4 Same percentage increase and decrease

If a value is first increased by a% and then decreased by a% (or vice versa), the net effect is always a decrease.

Net decrease = (a²/100)%
Example: Salary rises 5%, then falls 5%.
Net decrease = 5²/100 = 0.25%.
Check with ₹100: 100 → 105 → 99.75.

Technique 5 Two successive percentage changes

A value changes by a% and then by b%. Give + to increase and − to decrease.

Net change = [a + b + (a × b)/100]%
Keep the signs: e.g. +20% and −25% means a = +20, b = −25.
Example: Price increases 20%, then decreases 25%.
Net = 20 − 25 + (20 × −25)/100 = −5 − 5 = −10% (10% decrease).
Check: ₹100 → ₹120 → ₹90.

Technique 6 Price change & consumption to keep expenditure fixed

If price changes by a%, consumption must change in the opposite direction to keep expenditure unchanged.

Required consumption change = a/(100 ± a) × 100%
Price rises: reduction = a/(100+a) × 100%
Price falls: increase = a/(100−a) × 100%
Example: Price rises by 40%. Required reduction in consumption:
40/140 × 100 = 28 4/7%.
Example: Milk price falls by 20%. Increase consumption:
20/80 × 100 = 25%.

Technique 7 Marks, pass marks and failure

Let maximum marks = M, pass percentage = P%, and a candidate's marks = x.

Pass marks = M × P/100
Marks short of pass = Pass marks − x
Example: Maximum marks 200; pass mark 30%; student scores 30.
Pass marks = 200 × 30/100 = 60. Shortfall = 60 − 30 = 30 marks.

Fast track: If a student scores x% and fails by F marks, maximum marks:

M = 100F/(P − x)
Example: Pass mark 30%, score 15%, fail by 30 marks.
M = 100 × 30/(30 − 15) = 200.

Technique 8 Examination: scores and maximum marks

For a candidate scoring x% and another scoring y%, where the score difference is d marks:

Maximum marks = 100 × d/|x − y|
Example: One candidate scores 25%, another 50%; difference = 60 marks.
M = 100 × 60/(50 − 25) = 240 marks.

If a candidate scores x% and is a marks below another candidate who scores y%, use:

Maximum marks = 100 × a/(y − x)

Technique 9 Students failing in two subjects

If x% fail in subject A, y% fail in subject B, and z% fail in both:

Fail in at least one = (x + y − z)%
Pass in both = [100 − (x + y − z)]%
Example: 20% fail History, 15% fail Hindi, 5% fail both.
Fail in at least one = 20 + 15 − 5 = 30%.
Pass in both = 100 − 30 = 70%.

Technique 10 Price, quantity and expenditure

When price and quantity both change, expenditure is their product. Apply successive multipliers.

New expenditure = Old expenditure × (1 ± price%/100) × (1 ± quantity%/100)
Example: Price rises 10%, quantity falls 10%.
Net expenditure factor = 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99 ⇒ 1% decrease.

Sugar shortcut: If sugar price rises by 10%, ₹100 buys 100/110 kg of the old quantity; consumption reduction to maintain spending = 10/110 × 100 = 9 1/11%.

Technique 11 Election: two candidates

If the losing candidate gets x% of total votes and loses by y votes:

Total votes = (100 × y)/(100 − 2x)
Example 24: Losing candidate receives 30% and loses by 500 votes.
Total = 100 × 500/(100 − 60) = 50,000/40 = 1,250 votes.

Technique 12 Income / population compound change

If a quantity is P and increases/decreases at R% per annum:

After n years = P × (1 ± R/100)n
n years ago = P ÷ (1 + R/100)n (when it grew at R%)
Example 12: Monthly income ₹8,000 increases by 20%.
New income = 8,000 × 120/100 = ₹9,600.
Example 25: Population 352,800 grows 5% for 2 years.
After 2 years = 352,800 × 1.05² = 388,962.
2 years ago = 352,800 ÷ 1.05² = 320,000.

Technique 13 Different rates in different years

Apply the change for each year independently; multiply the yearly factors.

Final value = P × (1 ± R₁/100) × (1 ± R₂/100) × (1 ± R₃/100) …
Example 26: Population in 2004 = 10,00,000; 2005 +15%, 2006 −35%, 2007 +45%.
10,00,000 × 1.15 × 0.65 × 1.45 = 10,83,875.
